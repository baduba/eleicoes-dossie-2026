# References

## Electoral forensics literature
- Beber, B.; Scacco, A. (2012). What the Numbers Say: A Digit-Based Test for Election Fraud. *Political Analysis* 20(2): 211-234. [Cambridge](https://cambridge.org/core/product/AD86EEBC2F199E2C8A2FD36BD3799DF9)
- Deckert, J.; Myagkov, M.; Ordeshook, P. C. (2011). Benford's Law and the Detection of Election Fraud. *Political Analysis* 19(3): 245-268. doi:10.1093/pan/mpr014. Critique of the use of Benford in per-section vote counts.
- Mebane, W. R., Jr. (2011). Comment on "Benford's Law and the Detection of Election Fraud". *Political Analysis* 19(3): 269-272. doi:10.1093/pan/mpr024. Response to Deckert et al.: second-digit tests require expectations that account for the characteristics of ordinary elections.
- Mebane, W. R., Jr. (2008). Election Forensics: The Second-Digit Benford's Law Test and Recent American Presidential Elections. In Alvarez, Hall, Hyde (eds.), *Election Fraud*. Brookings. (confirmed only in a bibliographic listing)
- Klimek, P.; Yegorov, Y.; Hanel, R.; Thurner, S. (2012). Statistical detection of systematic election irregularities. *PNAS* 109(41): 16469-16473. doi:10.1073/pnas.1210722109.
- Kobak, D.; Shpilkin, S.; Pshenichnikov, M. S. (2016). Integer percentages as electoral falsification fingerprints. *Annals of Applied Statistics* 10(1): 54-73. doi:10.1214/16-AOAS904.

How they were used: Klimek (two-dimensional histogram of turnout vs. vote, H5); Beber and Scacco (last digit, H8); Kobak (integer percentages, H8); Deckert et al. and Mebane (rationale for not using Benford's first digit as evidence, H8).
