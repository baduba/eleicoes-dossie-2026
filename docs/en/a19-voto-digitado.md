# A19. Central blind spot: typed vote vs. recorded vote

> Public data verifies the chain from the voting machine's record to the final total. The correspondence between the typed vote and the recorded vote is verified only by sampling in the integrity test. The current model has no voter-verified record.

## 1. What public data does not verify

The RDV (digital vote record), the digital BU (poll-receipt), the printed BU, and the final tally are all generated by the **same voting machine software** (the law provides that the machine digitally record each vote and digitally sign the vote file: Lei 9.504/1997, art. 59, §§ 4 to 6, [text on Planalto](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9504compilado.htm); the machine's output files are listed in the official machine-file readme and in the TSE's IT Glossary). Therefore **A4, H9, and the cross-check against the printed BU do not verify whether the recorded vote matches the vote the voter typed**. They verify that the machine's artifacts are consistent with each other and with the total.

## 2. The 2026 integrity test

The integrity test is a mock election, with paper ballots filled out and then typed into the voting machine, on randomly drawn machines, to compare the typed vote with the recorded one. It is the **only** verification, in the current model, of the correspondence between typed vote and recorded vote.

**What the rule establishes.** Under art. 58 of Res. TSE 23.673/2021 (per external audit reports published by the TREs, [example: TRE-SC 2022](https://www.tre-sc.jus.br/eleicoes/arquivos_eleicoes/eleicoes-2022/audit_ele2022_2t_relatorio_auditoria_externa)), 23, 35, or 43 machines are drawn per UF (depending on whether the UF has up to 15,000, 15,001 to 30,000, or more than 30,000 sections), of which 20, 27, or 33 go into the integrity test; the biometrics-based test covers 5% to 10% of the machines in the integrity test (2024 external audit reports). Res. TSE 23.758/2026 formalized the biometrics-based integrity test in all UFs (indicated by a search result; the text was not opened). The sections are defined on the day before, with the participation of accredited institutions and, when necessary, by drawing (TCU); the TRE-MG presentation indicates one section per electoral zone and at least one from the state capital.

**National total.** Applying the rule to the per-UF section count from BWeb 2026, the integrity test would have **648 machines** in the 1st round (32 to 65 with biometrics). **This total was calculated from the rule; it is not a published number.** Confirmation in one case: TRE-SP reported 33 machines in the 2026 1st-round test, three of them with biometrics ([TRE-SP](https://www.tre-sp.jus.br/comunicacao/noticias/2026/Outubro/eleicoes-2026-teste-de-integridade-confirma-pleno-funcionamento-das-urnas-eletronicas); read in a search result). For 2022, the press recorded 641 machines, 58 of them with biometrics (statement attributed to Justice Moraes).

## 3. Statistical power of the integrity test

Probability that the test observes **at least one** divergence, if a fraction of the country's machines recorded a vote different from the one typed:

| fracao_de_urnas_alteradas | (a) n=648 (whole test) | (b) n=65 (biometrics only, 10%) | (b) n=32 (biometrics only, 5%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1% | 47,8% | 6,3% | 3,2% |
| 0.5% | 96,1% | 27,8% | 14,8% |
| 1% | 99,9% | 48,0% | 27,5% |
| 2% | 100,0% | 73,1% | 47,6% |
| 5% | 100,0% | 96,4% | 80,6% |
| 9% | 100,0% | 99,8% | 95,1% |

**Assumption (a).** A machine that recorded a vote different from the one typed would do so in the test as well: behavior in the test is the same as in the election, and the test machines are a random sample of the 499,248 voting machines (n = 648). **Assumption (b).** The software distinguishes the non-biometric test from the real election, and the only test indistinguishable from the real election is the one **with biometrics**; only those machines count (n = 32 to 65). The two (b) columns show the 5% and 10% limits. The calculations assume simple random sampling without replacement; the actual draw (one section per zone, at least one from the capital) is not exactly like that, so the numbers are approximations. This section describes only the test's detection capacity; it does not describe how an alteration would be constructed.

**Two different things.** (1) **Voter-verified record that stays in the machine:** the machine would print the vote, the voter would check it, and the paper would remain deposited in the machine, without leaving with the voter. This is what art. 5 of Lei 12.034/2009 and art. 59-A of Lei 9.504/1997 provided; both were declared unconstitutional. (2) **Receipt handed to the voter:** a paper that leaves the section with the voter, which would prove whom they voted for. The law in force provides for the signed digital record of each vote (art. 59, §§ 4 to 6) and the machine that "shall count each vote, ensuring its secrecy and inviolability" (art. 61), and does not provide for a paper receipt for the voter. The legal reason cited by the STF for barring the printed record is **ballot secrecy and freedom** (risk of coercion and of vote proof; in ADI 5889, also the fact that the process depended on verification by the voter). The reading that the reason for barring the receipt is to prevent vote proof, and therefore vote buying, follows from that STF reasoning; it is an inference made by this page, not a quoted passage.

## 5. Recount

There is no physical ballot in the current model; therefore there is no "paper recount". The possible "recount" is **reprocessing the RDV and the BUs** and comparing them with the final tally. This was done in [A4](resultados-fase2.md) (RDV recounted against the BU, Lula, opponent, blank votes, and total, in 14,067 sections in 2026) and in H9 (BWeb against `votacao_secao` and tally by zone and municipality), with no divergences in 2026. **This reprocessing uses files generated by the machine itself** and therefore does not measure the correspondence between the typed vote and the recorded vote (see section 1).

## 6. Conclusion of this section

Public data verifies the chain from the voting machine's record to the final total. The correspondence between the typed vote and the recorded vote is verified only by sampling in the integrity test. The current model has no voter-verified record.
